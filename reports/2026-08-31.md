### SWOT (preliminary, listing-only, not diligence)

- **Strengths:** 90+ years of continuous operation is a rare, hard-to-fake signal of durability in a specialty niche (woven textiles for light-industrial and technical apparel use) — this isn't a business that's had one good decade, it's had nine; long-standing customer and vendor relationships reduce the usual acquisition risk of a fragile, easily-poached client base; seller is offering some financing, which typically means they believe the business will keep performing after they leave; healthy DSCR cushion (2.42) gives real room for a rate increase or a soft first year without breaching covenants.
- **Weaknesses:** the owner currently works only 30–50% of the time in the business and is retiring — that's good news for absentee-friendliness, but it also means the listing doesn't disclose who actually runs day-to-day operations, and that person's retention through a change of ownership is unconfirmed; specialty woven textiles is a narrow, low-growth-by-nature niche — the 90-year tenure is a strength for stability but may also mean limited organic upside without deliberate expansion; no detail on customer concentration (how much revenue rides on the top 1–2 accounts) or on domestic vs. import sourcing exposure.
- **Opportunities:** technical apparel and light-industrial textiles is adjacent to several growing categories (PPE, workwear, outdoor/technical fabrics) a new owner could push into using the existing vendor base; nine decades of supplier relationships is a moat that's very hard for a new entrant to replicate, which could support a private-label or import-substitution play; minimal current owner involvement (30–50%) suggests there's room to professionalize sales/marketing without disrupting existing operations.
- **Threats:** textile manufacturing and distribution is exposed to tariff and import-cost volatility, which is a live policy risk right now rather than a hypothetical one; a business this old can also carry undisclosed legacy liabilities (pension-type obligations, deferred maintenance, aging IT/inventory systems) that a younger business wouldn't have — worth a harder look in diligence; the "owner is retiring" framing is common seller-motivation language and should be verified rather than taken as automatically positive (voluntary retirement vs. a business quietly declining under a checked-out owner can look identical from a listing alone).
